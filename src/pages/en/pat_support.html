Specialist professionals

These are the professionals who can support you. You can ask your ophthalmologist or GP to refer you.

  • Low vision optometrist: assesses how you see and fits aids and filters.
  • Rehabilitation worker: trains you in reading, orientation and mobility, and daily living skills.
  • Psychologist: supports you with the emotional impact of the diagnosis.
  • Social worker: advises on benefits, paperwork and resources.

We are preparing a validated directory of low vision specialists in Catalonia. Only professionals validated by the project team will be included.

Psychological support

A diagnosis can stir up a lot: sadness, fear of the future, anger, a sense of loss. Families can struggle too. Asking for help is not a sign of weakness, and you do not need to wait until you use a cane or feel very low.

  • Tell your GP: they can refer you to mental health services.
  • Look for psychologists experienced in sight loss or chronic conditions. The Official College of Psychology of Catalonia has a directory of professionals.
  • Associations and ONCE offer psychosocial support and groups.
  • Talking to people going through the same helps: see the peer support groups.

If you ever have thoughts of harming yourself, call 024 (Spain's free 24-hour suicide prevention line) or 112.

Rights, paperwork and social work

  • Disability certificate. You can apply to the Government of Catalonia for an assessment of your degree of disability. It gives access to benefits and concessions.
  • ONCE. If your sight is below specific limits (visual acuity of 0.1 or less, or a visual field of 10 degrees or less, in the better eye with correction), you can apply for membership.
  • Social work. Your primary care centre (CAP) and local social services can advise on grants for assistive products, long-term care, transport and workplace adjustments.
  • School and work. You are entitled to reasonable adjustments. See Schools and society.
