Children and young people at school

  • Explore with your child how they see best: distance, light, print size.
  • Work with the school: teachers need to know their needs (sitting near the board or away from the window, enlarged or digital materials, extra time in exams, use of a tablet or magnifier).
  • In Catalonia, pupils with visual impairment can receive support from specialist educational teams. Ask the school or ONCE.
  • Encourage play, sport and activities with other children: independence is learned by doing.
  • In adolescence, how they feel in front of others matters a lot. Talking openly with the class helps.

The white cane: making it normal

The white cane is not only for people who are totally blind. Many people with low vision use one:

  • only at night or in poor light;
  • in unfamiliar or crowded places;
  • to detect steps and obstacles they cannot see;
  • so that others know they see poorly.

Using it on and off is normal. For many people, starting to use a cane is a hard step: comments like “you didn't use one before” make it harder. Respecting it, not touching it and not questioning it is the best help.

A society that understands low vision

  • {{icon:users}} In the street and in shops

    Offer help without imposing it. Read prices or signs if asked. Say who you are when you speak.

  • {{icon:pro}} At work

    Screens with magnification or a screen reader, good lighting, accessible digital documents and flexibility. Many adjustments are simple and cheap.

  • {{icon:phone}} Online

    Accessible websites and apps, alternative text for images and audio-described videos benefit everyone.
