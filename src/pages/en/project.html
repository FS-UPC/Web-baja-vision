The challenge

Low vision is a health, inclusion and equity challenge that is growing as the population ages. Beyond the clinical condition, people with low vision struggle to access reliable, accessible information, suitable support resources and ongoing contact with specialist professionals.

These difficulties are made worse by fragmented resources, a lack of accessible and validated digital solutions, and the gap between academic knowledge, professional practice and society.

Goal

To develop, validate and transfer an accessible digital platform that turns specialist knowledge about low vision into a usable, structured and socially relevant resource, able to improve the independence of people with low vision and strengthen professional practice in eye health.

Phases and timeline

The project runs for 18 months in three consecutive phases, plus a cross-cutting management and communication phase.

  1. Phase 0 · Launch and project management

    Months 1 to 18

    Management structure, roles, communication channels, dissemination plan and participation mechanisms for partner organisations.

  2. Phase 1 · Needs analysis, co-creation and functional definition

    Months 1 to 6

    Needs analysis with a diverse range of users and professionals, co-creation sessions and functional definition of the platform.

    Deliverables: needs analysis report; functional definition document.

  3. Phase 2 · Design and development of the accessible digital solution

    Months 7 to 14

    Information architecture, accessible technical development, plain-language content creation and iterative validation.

    Deliverables: functional, accessible platform; structured content; functional validation report.

  4. Phase 3 · Validation, use and knowledge transfer

    Months 15 to 18

    Real-world validation with people with low vision, final adjustments, documentation of lessons learned, and transfer and dissemination activities.

    Deliverables: validated platform; transfer and dissemination materials; lessons-learned report; final report.

Co-creation sessions (November 2026)

On 19 and 20 November 2026, at the headquarters of the Official College of Opticians and Optometrists of Catalonia (COOOC), two focus group sessions were held with 9 people with very diverse profiles: from legal blindness with intensive use of screen readers and braille displays to functional low vision, photophobia, central or peripheral field loss and complications after surgery. Their conclusions, together with the principal investigator's strategic priorities, have shaped the current structure of this website.

Four principles

  1. Universal, customisable accessibility (WCAG 2.2 AA/AAA): compatibility with screen readers and braille displays, and two-way contrast (dark mode and light high-contrast mode), because a single mode disadvantages some people.
  2. Three main areas and an assistant (two-click rule): Patients; Family and community; Professionals.
  3. Realistic empathy: neither defeatist messages nor toxic positivity; real testimonials, myths and facts, and what can be done.
  4. Active clinical referral: downloadable tools with checkboxes and QR codes for professionals to hand out in consultations.

What we learned

  • The post-diagnosis gap: some people took between 2 and 38 years to learn about optical aids and rehabilitation.
  • Lack of sensitivity in some consultations and late referrals from primary care.
  • Misunderstanding of mixed vision and intermittent cane use.
  • Rejection of toxic positivity: people asked for a warm, hopeful and realistic tone.
  • Opposite visual needs: some people need a dark background and others a light one, and text must be enlargeable without distorting icons.

Website roadmap

  1. Architecture and accessibility: semantic structure, built-in reader and testing of the contrast bar in light and dark modes.
  2. Content and tools: content for the three areas, the resource assistant and the PDF and QR generator for consultations.
  3. Participatory testing: testing with the 9 participants (assistive technology compatibility and legibility) before public launch. Take part.

How we work

  • {{icon:users}} Co-creation

    People with low vision, professionals and organisations take part from the start in defining needs, content and features.

  • {{icon:check}} Iterative validation

    Every stage is tested with users to find barriers and fix them early.

  • {{icon:lang}} Catalan as source language

    Sessions, documents and content are created in Catalan, in plain and easy-read language, and reviewed by the UPC Language and Terminology Service.

  • {{icon:scale}} Gender perspective and inclusion

    Participants diverse in gender, age, level of sight and digital skills, with attention to socio-economic and digital inequalities.

  • {{icon:leaf}} Sustainability

    Lightweight digital architecture, open standards, focus on essential features and fewer printed materials.

  • {{icon:book}} Open science

    Publicly accessible content and materials, anonymised and aggregated data, and shared lessons learned to encourage reuse.

Expected impact

{{icon:person}} People with low vision

  • Greater independence in daily life.
  • Better ability to make informed decisions about eye health.
  • Fewer barriers to specialist information.
  • Better perceived quality of life.

{{icon:pro}} Professionals

  • Access to training resources and structured materials.
  • Better coordination between health and social care.
  • More consistent support for people with low vision.

{{icon:network}} System and society

  • A replicable model of knowledge transfer in eye health.
  • Content that can be reused and adapted to other regions.
  • More awareness and less stigma.

Impact will be measured with indicators on platform use, satisfaction among users and professionals, perceived independence (before and after), transfer activities carried out and use of content in Catalan.

Track record

The project builds on the service-learning work in eye health of the Faculty of Optics and Optometry of Terrassa (FOOT) through the CUV. Since 2007, the Mirades Solidàries programme has provided eye care to more than 40,000 people across Catalonia, over 30,000 of them in vulnerable situations, working with around 70 local authorities and third-sector organisations.

The CUV also coordinated the project “Aprenentatge i servei en gestió de la miopia” (service-learning in myopia management), funded under the AGAUR IMPACTE 2023 call.
