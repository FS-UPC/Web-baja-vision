Classification of visual impairment

The WHO International Classification of Diseases (ICD-11) classifies distance visual impairment by presenting visual acuity in the better eye:

Categories of distance visual impairment (ICD-11, WHO)
CategoryVisual acuity worse thanVisual acuity equal to or better than
Mild6/12 (0.5 decimal)6/18 (0.3)
Moderate6/18 (0.3)6/60 (0.1)
Severe6/60 (0.1)3/60 (0.05)

Near visual impairment is defined as near visual acuity worse than N6 (0.8 M) with existing correction. Visual acuity does not describe all visual function: visual field, contrast sensitivity, glare and the person's needs are equally decisive.

Functional low vision assessment

The aim is not just to measure, but to understand what the person wants to do and what prevents them:

  1. Functional history and goals

    Diagnosis and progression, treatments, activities given up, environment, digital skills, social support and expectations. Prioritise concrete goals.

  2. Distance and near visual acuity

    With low vision charts (logMAR, e.g. ETDRS type) at reduced distances if needed. Record reading acuity and reading speed.

  3. Refraction

    Careful refraction with adapted techniques often brings improvements that had been missed.

  4. Contrast sensitivity and glare

    For example with the Pelli-Robson chart. Assess response to different lighting levels and the value of selective filters.

  5. Visual field and scotomas

    Peripheral and central fields (Amsler grid, perimetry), locating scotomas and the preferred retinal locus to guide eccentric viewing training.

  6. Magnification prediction and trial of aids

    Initial magnification estimate (e.g. Kestenbaum's rule: addition in dioptres ≈ reciprocal of decimal distance acuity) and real-world trial of optical, electronic and technological aids against goals.

  7. Training, follow-up and referral

    Training plan, lighting and environment advice, and coordination with rehabilitation, social work, psychology and associations.

Accessible communication

  • Introduce yourself by name on entering and say when you leave. Address the person, not their companion.
  • Describe what you are doing during the examination and before touching.
  • Provide information in large print (at least 16–18 pt, sans serif, good contrast) or an accessible digital format, such as the resource pack generator.
  • Many older people with low vision also have hearing loss: reduce noise, face them, with light on your face.
