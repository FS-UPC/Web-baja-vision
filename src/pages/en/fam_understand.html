Understanding low vision

Low vision cannot be seen from the outside. Understanding how the other person sees avoids misunderstandings and helps you support them better.

Mixed vision and intermittent cane use

One of the most frequent complaints in the co-creation sessions was being misunderstood by others. Some situations that may seem contradictory but are not:

  • She reads her phone but walks into doors. She may keep central vision while having lost side vision.
  • He doesn't say hello in the street. If he cannot see the centre, he may not recognise your face. It is not that he does not want to greet you.
  • No cane by day, a cane at night. In poor light many people see much less. Using a cane only when needed is normal.
  • One day they can do something, another day they can't. Vision changes with light, tiredness and mood. They are not exaggerating.

Comments like “you're so absent-minded” or “I saw you without your cane” hurt. If in doubt, ask.

Differences from other disabilities

Low vision, blindness and other disabilities: what to bear in mind
SituationWhat it meansWhat helps
Low visionSees partly. May read with aids, but with difficulty depending on light and distance.Contrast, good light, large print, patience and not moving things.
Legal blindnessSight is very reduced, but often some perception of light, shapes or a very small field remains.Cane or guide dog, screen reader, braille, verbal descriptions.
Total blindnessNo useful visual perception.Spoken or tactile information, braille, predictable surroundings.
Visible disabilities (for example, wheelchair users)Others recognise the disability straight away.Physical accessibility.

Low vision is often an invisible disability: because it does not show, the person constantly has to explain. Making that easier is a great help.

Ask, don't assume

Two people with the same condition may see very differently. The best way to find out is to ask naturally:

  • “What can you see well and what is hard?”
  • “Would you like me to help, or would you rather do it yourself?”
  • “How should I warn you about a step or an obstacle?”
  • “What lighting works best for you?”
