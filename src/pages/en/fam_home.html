Adapting the home and lighting

Small changes, without building work, can make a big difference to safety and independence. Make them together, respecting the way the person organises things.

On this page

Light

  • Even throughout the home, with no dark corners or sudden changes between rooms.
  • Task lighting where they read, in the kitchen and bathroom, pointed at the task, not the eyes.
  • No glare: sheer curtains or blinds to control sunlight, bulbs that are not seen directly, matt surfaces.
  • Hallways and stairs well lit, with switches that are easy to find or motion sensors.
  • If the person has photophobia, they may prefer less general light and more task light. Ask them.

Contrast

  • Contrasting tape on step edges.
  • Switches, sockets and handles that stand out from the wall.
  • Dark plates for light food and light plates for dark food; light and dark chopping boards.
  • Towels and mats in colours that contrast with the floor and walls.

Order and clear paths

  • Everything always in the same place. If you must move something, say so. This is one of the most important tips.
  • Remove rugs that cause trips, cables and objects from the floor.
  • Doors and drawers fully open or fully closed, never half-way.
  • Chairs always pushed in under the table.

Glossary of practical tips

Tips sorted by place or activity.

{{icon:home}} Bathroom
Non-slip mat in the shower, grab rails if needed and products in different colours or with tactile markers.
{{icon:check}} Appliance buttons
Raised adhesive dots on the most-used settings of the oven, washing machine or microwave.
{{icon:light}} Kitchen
Under-cabinet lighting, utensils in contrasting colours and a fixed place for everything.
{{icon:text}} Labels
Large print, raised markers or elastic bands to tell jars, spices and cleaning products apart.
{{icon:heart}} Medicines
Large pill organisers, clear labels and, if needed, apps that read the box.
{{icon:phone}} Phones and remotes
Models with large buttons and, if needed, voice control.
{{icon:walk}} Front door
A fixed spot for keys, cane and glasses; a light by the door.
{{icon:book}} Clothes
Organised by colour or with tactile markers; offer help matching outfits if the person wants it.

Older people

  • Sight loss increases the risk of falls. Check lighting and obstacles at home.
  • Many older people with low vision see things that are not there. This is Charles Bonnet syndrome: it is not dementia. Talk about it openly and mention it to their eye care professional.
  • Watch out for isolation: keeping up activities and contacts matters as much as adapting the home.
