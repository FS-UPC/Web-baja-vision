What people told us in the sessions

In November 2026, nine people with low vision or blindness took part in two co-creation sessions for this website. Here are some of the ideas they shared, summarised in our own words and anonymously:

  • It took me many years to find out that optical aids existed. Nobody had told me about them.

  • During the day I go without a cane and at night I need it. People don't understand and tell me they saw me without it.

  • I can read small print, yet I walk into doors. People say I'm absent-minded.

  • I don't want “you can do anything” messages. I want to know what I can do and how.

  • Dark mode saves me; for someone else in the group it makes it impossible to see. We need to be able to choose.

  • I wish I'd had a psychologist from the start, not only once I was already using a cane.

Peer support groups

Sharing experiences with others is one of the most valued forms of support. There are formal groups run by organisations and informal ones.

  • {{icon:users}} Association groups

    Associació Discapacitat Visual Catalunya: B1+B2+B3 organises activities, meetings and peer support.

  • {{icon:heart}} Condition-specific associations

    Many conditions (retinitis pigmentosa, glaucoma, AMD, rare diseases) have patient associations with groups and research news. Ask your professional or the association.

  • {{icon:chat}} Informal groups

    Messaging groups, social media or local meet-ups. Useful for everyday tips; always check medical information with a professional.

  • {{icon:network}} ONCE

    If you are a member, ONCE also offers activities and groups.

Would you like to share your experience?

If you would like to record a testimonial to help others, contact the University Vision Centre. Always with your consent, and anonymously if you prefer.
